# Beyond "religion": a taxonomy for Globasa

*Research notes of 23 November 2026, compiled for the Globasa lexicon team. A companion to
[conceptual_parity.md](conceptual_parity.md) and [spirit_beings.md](spirit_beings.md). Every
word form here is a proposal, and none has been through `globasa_collision_check.py`. The
sources in §12 are cited from memory and must be checked before publication.*

## Summary

The English word "religion" bundles several things that most traditions keep apart: honouring
particular beings, following a teaching, belonging to a community, holding a picture of the
world, and telling sacred stories. It also assumes exclusive membership, which fits
Christianity and Islam far better than a Buddhist who honours the village god, or a Stoic who
sacrificed in the city's temples.

This note proposes a small set of Globasa terms that separate those dimensions:

| Term | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| ***kultucwenton*** | an inherited, practice-centred tradition (the trunk) | new compound |
| ***sekularcwenton*** | an inherited secular background: the scientific worldview assumed, the cult of beings receded | new compound |
| ***fikirxube*** | a school of thought (a branch) | new compound |
| ***kultuxube*** | a cult lineage (a branch) | new compound |
| ***dini*** | a school fused with a cult that is complete for its members | regloss |
| ***dunyadini*** | "world religion", the conventional list | new compound |
| ***kultu*** | the honouring of a particular being | regloss |
| ***teolari***, ***hikayelari***, ***kosmologi*** | the gods as a set, the stories as a set, the worldview | new / existing |
| ***minxikultu***, ***dawlakultu***, ***xaherkultu*** | civic, state and city cult | new compounds |

## 1. The problem with "religion"

- **The category is modern.** W. C. Smith, Asad, Masuzawa and Nongbri trace "religion" as a bounded,
  belief-centred box to early modern Protestant Europe and the 19th-century "world religions"
  list. Japanese 宗教 was coined in the Meiji period; Shinto was then declared "not a religion".
- **The traditions had other categories.**
  - China: the 三教, "Three Teachings", used together, over a shared popular cult.
  - Japan: 神仏習合, kami and buddhas combined; local kami as traces of buddhas (*honji suijaku*).
  - India: *dharma*, *sampradāya* (lineage), *mārga* (path).
  - Rome: Varro's three theologies: mythical (the poets), physical (the philosophers), civil
    (the city's cult).
  - Indonesia: *agama* (recognised scriptural religion) against *adat* (inherited custom).
- **Scholars model religion in layers.** Spiro's three Buddhisms, Tambiah's "total field", Chau's
  modalities and "ritual polytropy", C. K. Yang's diffused versus institutional religion,
  Hadot's philosophy as a way of life.

## 2. The trunk and the branches

The core of the proposal is one image, carried by existing Globasa roots: an inherited
**trunk** and the **branches** that grow from it (*xube* "branch", as in *dinixube* "sect",
*basaxube* "dialect").

### *kultucwenton*: the inherited tradition

*kultu* "cult" + *cwenton* "tradition". An inherited, cumulative tradition centred on practice:
honouring gods, ancestors and spirits, with its own gods, stories and worldview. It has no
founder, is tied to a people and a land, belonging comes through taking part, and it lives
alongside schools.

- **Examples:** Greek and Roman religion, Shinto, Hinduism, Chinese popular religion, the Vedic and
  Indo-Iranian traditions, Yoruba religion, many Indigenous traditions.
- **The traditions' own names point at inheritance:** Greek *ta patria*, Latin *mos maiorum*,
  Hindu *paramparā*.
- **Rejected forms:** *warisadao* (*dao* "way" overlaps with schools as "ways of life");
  *dinikanu* ("religion trunk", which brings back *dini*); a loan of *adat* (too close to
  *adate* "adaptation", and a plain "habit" outside Malay).

### *fikirxube*: the school of thought

*fikir* "thought" + *xube* "branch". A school, usually founded, that grows within an inherited
tradition or against it.

- **Examples:** the Stoics, Epicureans and Pyrrhonists; the six *darśana*s; Buddhism; Taoism
  (道家 and 道教); Confucianism (儒家); the Pharisees and Sadducees (Josephus' *haireseis*);
  Cārvāka; modern scientific skepticism.
- **Chinese marks the distinction:** 家 "school" against 教 "teaching as an institution".
- **Related words:** *-ismo* names a doctrine (*Stoaismo*); *-ista* an adherent; *eskol* the
  institution, where there was one (the Academy, the Garden).
- **Rejected forms:** *sofixube* (philosophy only); loans of *madhhab* (drifted to "religion"
  and "sect") or 學派 (Sinosphere only).

### Two kinds of branch: *kultuxube*

Beside the *fikirxube*, a branch defined by teaching, a tradition has the ***kultuxube***, a
branch defined by **cult**: a lineage of honouring one being, its shrines and rites.

- **Examples:** Shaiva, Vaishnava and Shakta lineages; the Greek mystery cults (Eleusis,
  Dionysus); Yoruba *òrìṣà* houses.
- **Shrine networks that branch literally:** a new Mazu or Inari shrine is founded by 分香/分靈,
  "dividing the incense or the spirit" from a mother temple.
- **Modern forms:** New Age is a practice-centred, eclectic *kultuxube* on a *sekularcwenton*
  (Heelas; Hanegraaff). Western esotericism also has a strong doctrinal side ("rejected
  knowledge"), so it is partly a *fikirxube*. Neo-paganism reconstructs its trunk.
- **Either background:** a *fikirxube* works on a *kultucwenton* or a *sekularcwenton* alike.

### Two relations, and a cycle

A school has two relations to a tradition: **where it arose** and **what surrounds it now**.

| School | Arose in | Surrounded by |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | the Vedic *kultucwenton* | the local one wherever it went (Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Tibetan) |
| Taoism | the Chinese *kultucwenton* | the same |
| Stoicism | the Greek *kultucwenton* | the same |

The relations change over time. A tradition gives rise to schools; some schools become *dini*;
a *dini* in turn becomes an inherited background for later generations (cultural Christians).
The terms are relations at a given time, not fixed types.

## 3. *dini*: fusion

**Definition (team decision, 2026-11-23):** a *dini* is a *fikirxube* and a *kultucwenton*
fused: the teaching governs the cult, and the cult is **complete enough to replace a
*kultucwenton*** for its members (worship, festivals, rites from birth to death).
**Exclusivity is a common consequence, not part of the definition.**

- **Exclusive *dini*:** Christianity, Islam, political religions; probably Jōdo Shinshū.
  Shinran held that relying on kami betrays reliance on "self-power" instead of Amida's
  "other-power". Later Shin teaching formalised this as 神祇不拝, "non-worship of the kami",
  but sources differ on how strictly it was applied. Check the primary texts (the
  *Kyōgyōshinshō*, the *Shōzōmatsu wasan*, Rennyo's letters) before relying on this case.
- **Inclusive *dini*:** Cao Đài, Yiguandao, Tenrikyō, Bahá'í.
- **In between:** Sikhism; Mazdayasna (Zoroastrianism) today, exclusive in cult but not
  missionary.

**Why Buddhism stays a *fikirxube*:** it supplies only part of its members' cult life. In Japan,
people are "born Shinto, die Buddhist"; Theravāda villagers go to the spirit cults for
protection and to the monks for merit.

**Christianity shows the path:** a *hairesis* within the Jewish *kultucwenton* (Acts 24:5)
became a *dini* through fusion with its own cult and through mission.

**Mazdayasna is the classic case.** Zarathustra's teaching arose within the Indo-Iranian tradition,
fused with its cult in the Younger Avesta, and gained state backing under the Sasanians. Its
self-name, *Mazdayasna*, "worship of Mazdā", names the cult; "Zoroastrianism" names the
founder. **Verified:** Jeffery (*The Foreign Vocabulary of the Qurʾān*, p. 131ff.) splits
Qurʾānic *dīn* into two words: "religion" from Middle Persian *dēn* (< Avestan *daēnā*), and
"judgment" from Aramaic *dīnā*. Nöldeke, Vollers and Cheung (2017) agree; Gaudefroy-Demombynes
and Gardet were not convinced. If Jeffery is right, Globasa *dini* goes back to a Zoroastrian
word.

**Judaism tests every term:** a *kultucwenton* (the Temple cult, then prayer as *avodah*) with a path
(*halakha*, "the going") and stories (*aggadah*), schools (Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes),
modern denominations (*dinixube*), and peoplehood. Batnitzky shows that "Judaism as a
religion" is a 19th-century framing.

### Backward compatibility

The corpus uses *dini* in the general sense: the UDHR (*huruya fe fikir, yoxin ji dini*),
*dinidom* "church, temple", *dinili grupo*. Under the narrow definition those passages would
protect or describe only *dini* proper.

- **Proposed:** ***dunyadini*** "world religion", the conventional list (Christianity, Islam,
  Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Sikhism…), a calque of *Weltreligion* / 世界宗教 / *agama dunia*.
- **Still open:** whether *dini* keeps a general second sense for legacy and legal texts, or a new
  generic word is needed (a candidate: *santocwenton*, "sacred tradition"). *dunyadini* can't
  serve the UDHR, because it would exclude minority and Indigenous traditions.

## 4. The parts of a tradition

| Term | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ***kultu*** | the honouring of a particular being: worship, veneration, offerings, rites, shrines, festivals (*kultu de Apolo*, *Inari kultu*, *lefeatrekultu* "ancestor cult") | regloss: remove the pejorative 邪教 and *Sekte* from its columns |
| ***ibada*** | worship proper | too strong for saints, ancestors or civic rites |
| (veneration) | honouring without worship (*dulia*) | gap |
| ***gixiki*** | one rite | exists |
| ***teolari*** | the gods of a tradition as a set (*teo* + *lari* "collection", as in *jawherilari*) | new; not *-mon*, which forms a single piece |
| ***hikayelari*** | the stories as a set: *aggadah*, the Purāṇas, Dreaming stories | new; neutral |
| ***kosmologi*** | the worldview | exists |
| ***mito*** / ***mitologi***, ***afsane*** | "myth", "legend" | use only where the source does: they imply falsehood |

**Watch 信仰:** with a being's name (稲荷信仰, 媽祖信仰) it means a cult, so *kultu*; as personal
conviction it means *xinen*.

## 5. Civic and state cult, and nationalism

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ***minxikultu*** | civic cult (*minxi* "civic") | Bellah's civil religion: flags, anthems, memorials, national days |
| ***dawlakultu*** | cult run by a state | Confucian state rites; State Shinto (declared "not a religion") |
| ***xaherkultu*** | city cult | Greek polis cult |
| ***nasyonismo*** | nationalism as a doctrine, a *fikirxube* | — |

- **Political religion** (Voegelin, Gentile) is a *dini* by the fusion definition: fascism, Juche, the
  Soviet leader cult.
- **The personified nation shows the background deciding:** Marianne stays an allegory on a
  secular background, while Bharat Mata gets a temple on the Hindu one.

## 6. Secular backgrounds, science and skepticism

- **Atheism** (*teokalismo*) is a thesis held by schools. Atheist schools have grown on traditions:
  Cārvāka, Xunzi (who kept the rites while denying the spirits), nearly the Epicureans.
- **The reverse is common:** a tradition without a school. Japanese 無宗教 ("no religion") people
  still visit shrines; cultural Christians and secular Jews keep the festivals.
- ***sekularcwenton*:** an inherited background in which the scientific *kosmologi* is taken
  for granted and the cult of beings has receded (Taylor's "immanent frame"). It is more than
  *syensi*:

  | Part | In a *sekularcwenton* | Is it *syensi*? |
  |---|---|---|
  | *kosmologi* | the scientific picture, held in a popular form | yes |
  | efficacy | technology, medicine | yes, applied |
  | *hikayelari* | the story of progress, national histories, Big History | partly |
  | ethics | dignity, rights, individualism | no: largely inherited (Taylor; Siedentop; Holland) |
  | calendar | the week, Sunday, Christmas as a public holiday | no: inherited |
  | honouring | *minxikultu* | no |

  Methodological naturalism is *syensi*; metaphysical physicalism is a school (*maderiismo*).
  A *sekularcwenton* is usually the **successor of a particular *kultucwenton***: Western
  secularity is post-Christian (Taylor; Asad, *Formations of the Secular*), Japanese secularity
  keeps ancestor rites and shrine visits. So there are several secularities (Eisenstadt's
  "multiple modernities"): "the Japanese *sekularcwenton*", "the European one".
- **Grand narratives** (***dayhikaye***, after Lyotard's *grand récit*) are part of a background's
  *hikayelari*. The *sekularcwenton*'s own is the story of progress (***fronkadam dayhikaye***):
  Whig history (Butterfield, 1931), which Löwith read as secularised Christian eschatology
  (Blumenberg disagreed). Other traditions tell time in other shapes:

  | Shape | Examples |
  |---|---|
  | progress | Whig history, Marx's stages, the techno-singularity |
  | decline | Hesiod's metal ages, the Confucian golden age, Buddhist 末法 *mappō* (the basis of Shinran's teaching) |
  | cycle | the Hindu *yuga*s, the Chinese dynastic cycle, the Stoic conflagration, Ibn Khaldun, the Aztec Suns |
  | toward an end | Zoroastrian world-renewal (*frašō.kərəti*), then Jewish, Christian and Islamic history (Cohn) |
  | not linear | the Dreaming as "everywhen" (Stanner) |

- **Postmodernism** is two things. ***xafemodernismo*** is a *fikirxube* on the *sekularcwenton*
  (Lyotard, Foucault, Derrida, Rorty) that questions that background's grand narrative and
  science's authority. Its critics note that "incredulity toward grand narratives" is itself one
  (Habermas). ***xafemodernya*** is a condition of the background: many stories, none in charge,
  and pick-and-mix practice (Jameson, Harvey). This note's own critique of "religion"
  (Masuzawa, Asad, Josephson) descends from postmodern genealogy (*xafe-* "after"; Globasa
  *pos-* negates).
- **A successful school can fade into a secular background.** On a *sekularcwenton*, a
  *fikirxube* can be absorbed whole: its theses stop being argued and become assumed
  (liberalism; Marxism-Leninism in post-Soviet societies; Darwinian naturalism). On a
  *kultucwenton* the background slot is taken. Buddhism and Taoism stay visible as institutions
  inside it (monks, priests, temples), while their ideas seep in (karma and 因果, yin-yang, 氣,
  fengshui).
- **Secularism as politics** (*laïcité*) is a state arrangement, not a worldview: ***sekularismo***.
- **NOMA** (Gould) is a school proposing a treaty between science and "religion". It only fits a
  *dini* that has given up its *kosmologi*, never a *kultucwenton* (Evans-Pritchard,
  Malinowski, Horton, Harrison, Asad).
- **Science and technology** supply the secular background's *kosmologi* and much of *kultu*'s
  practical efficacy. Where uncertainty remains, cult persists: priests bless Soyuz rockets,
  Japan holds AIBO funerals.
- **Skepticism:** ***xankaismo*** / ***xankaista***. Ancient Pyrrhonism lived inside a
  *kultucwenton*; modern scientific skepticism (Randi, Mick West) guards the secular
  background's edges. Debunking is old: Lucian, Wang Chong, Inoue Enryō's *yōkaigaku*.

## 7. Magic, religion and science

"Magic" is mostly a boundary drawn by an authority, not a kind of thing (J. Z. Smith, Styers,
Otto and Stausberg). The word itself comes from the *magi*, the Persian priests. In Globasa:

- **Neutral descriptions:** the practical, results-seeking side of *kultu*; *mansi* "divination";
  *jumun* "spell".
- **Judgment words, used only where a source judges:** *mixin* "superstition" (from 迷信, a Meiji
  boundary word); *burdinixube* "bad sect" for 邪教; *jumunya* as illicit sorcery.
- **Native categories to respect:** Arabic separates *muʿjiza*, *karāma* and *siḥr* by the source of
  the power; Chinese 方術 never drew a magic/science line.

### The source of power

| Source | Examples | Globasa (proposals) |
|---|---|---|
| innate | Azande witchcraft, second sight, 霊感; mutants, quirks | ***xenkowa*** "born power" |
| acquired by technique | sorcery, 修仙 cultivation, Buddhist 神通 | ***jumunya*** (narrowed) |
| granted by a being | *muʿjiza*, *karāma*, charisma, the shaman's call, pacts | ***hadyakowa*** "gift power" |
| technological or accidental | Iron Man, Spider-Man | — |

- **Superpower, psychic power:** ***ultra-abilya*** (after 超能力). **Superhero:** ***ultrahero***.
- **Still a gap:** the spirit medium.

## 8. Fiction

- **Fantasy** (***fantasifiksyon***) keeps a tradition's stories and beings without the cult,
  told "as if": Tolkien's sub-creation and secondary belief.
- **Invented worlds** have their own *teolari*, *kosmologi* and *dini*, and the same terms translate
  them.
- **Fiction can become cult:** Jediism, the Church of All Worlds, Japanese anime pilgrimage
  (*anime zyaratu*).
- **Science fiction and fantasy** share the aim of controlling the world. They differ by
  background *kosmologi*: causality against participation (Tambiah). Chinese genre names sort by
  borrowed tradition: 科幻, 奇幻, 玄幻, 仙侠, 武侠.
- **Fiction shapes belief.** Stories feed back into what people believe:
  - **Nessie:** modern sightings begin in 1933, the year *King Kong* showed a long-necked creature
    in the water.
  - **Dinosaur cryptids keep the science of their decade:** Mokele-mbembe as an upright,
    tail-dragging sauropod; Nessie as a swan-necked plesiosaur. The outdated reconstruction dates
    the belief (Loxton and Prothero, *Abominable Science!*, 2013).
  - **The historical twin, inside a *dini*:** Dante's *Inferno* (hell's ordered circles) and
    Milton's *Paradise Lost* (Satan's rebellion, the war in heaven, "Pandaemonium") shape
    popular Christian imagery more than scripture's sparse account does.
  - **The yōkai catalogue** (Toriyama Sekien, Mizuki) now defines how many Japanese picture
    yōkai.

## 9. Status of this taxonomy

This taxonomy is a **metamodern reconstruction**. It follows the genealogies of "religion" to
take the category apart (Masuzawa, Asad, Josephson, Batnitzky), then rebuilds terms tested
against ethnography.

It doesn't claim to be the one true classification. Several taxonomies can fit the evidence
equally well:
- Dupré's "promiscuous realism": many legitimate classifications, each good for a purpose;
- underdetermination (Duhem, Quine);
- Putnam's conceptual relativity; Goodman's *Ways of Worldmaking*;
- Berlin's value pluralism: competing criteria can have several best trade-offs.

**Criteria used here:** fit to observation, translatability, religious parity, economy of roots.
The forms set aside in §2 (*warisadao*, *dinikanu*, *lefeatredao*, an *adat* loan) are
**alternative maximizers** under other weightings, kept on record so the team can weigh them
differently.

## 10. Open decisions

1. *dini*: keep a general second sense for legacy and legal texts, or add a generic word
   (*santocwenton*?).
2. The forms *kultucwenton*, *fikirxube*, *kultuxube*, *sekularcwenton*, *dunyadini*, *teolari*,
   *hikayelari*, *minxikultu*, *nasyonismo*, *xenkowa*, *hadyakowa*, *ultra-abilya*,
   *fantasifiksyon*, *dayhikaye*, *xafemodernismo*, *xafemodernya*: all need the collision check.
3. The *kultu* regloss, and removing 邪教 and *Sekte* from its columns.
4. The veneration word (*dulia*), and the spirit medium.
5. Placing Jainism (likely a *fikirxube*), Judaism, Sikhism and Daoist organisations case by case.
6. Splitting *jumunya* (learned) from *xenkowa* (innate), and the stage-magic sense of *magika*.
