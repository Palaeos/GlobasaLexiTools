# Spirit beings in Globasa: a comparative vocabulary

*Research notes of 23 November 2026, compiled for the Globasa lexicon team. This continues
[conceptual_parity.md](conceptual_parity.md) (§§6–11, souls and *ruho*). Every word form here
is a proposal: nothing has been changed in the word list, and no form has yet been through
`globasa_collision_check.py`. The sources listed in §15 are cited from memory and must be
checked before anything is published.*

## Summary

Globasa currently names supernatural beings with English-shaped words: *ruho* "spirit, soul",
*xabahu* "ghost", *demoni*, *kayvutu* "monster" (whose Chinese column lists 妖怪), *peari*
"fairy", *ninfa*, *elfu*, *dwarfu*, *kentawro*, *anjilyen*. It has the Greco-Germanic fantasy
bestiary as roots, and *jini* for jinn, but no word for the nāga, apsaras, tengu, a spirit medium or an
owner spirit.

The proposal:

- **One hypernym**, *ruhoyen* "spirit being", with *ruho* kept for spirit as substance and
  ethos (§2).
- **Slots defined by a being's relation to people**, not by what it is made of: worshipped,
  owner of a domain, anomalous, attendant, the dead (§3).
- **Named classes** from particular traditions (*ninfa*, *yaksa*, nāga, jinn) placed under the
  slot their own tradition gives them (§9).
- **Scope by ontology** (Descola): the 妖怪 slot belongs to analogist cosmologies; animist
  ontologies are served by grammar (*te*) and owner spirits instead (§4, §10).

## 1. Principles

1. **Native usage.** A meaning follows how source-language speakers use a word, not English
   glosses (zh.wiktionary renders 妖怪 in English as *demon, monster, spirit, elf*: an example
   of the drift to avoid).
2. **Sprachbund-wide or recurring.** A loan needs a Sprachbund-wide attestation; a concept
   that recurs across unrelated families is coined as a derivation or calque.
3. **Relational definitions.** What a being *is* depends on its relation to people: Wolf's
   gods/ghosts/ancestors mirror officials/strangers/kin; Komatsu's yōkai is an unworshipped
   kami; Avestan *yazata* literally means "worthy of worship".
4. **Polythetic classes** (Needham 1975). Cross-cultural categories are family resemblances,
   not necessary-and-sufficient definitions; borderline cases score lower on some features.
5. **Religious parity.** No tradition's beings are filed under another tradition's theology:
   jinn are not gods, yazatas are not angels, a transcendent Creator is not one spirit among many.
6. **Names over concepts only where the concept is a named class.** A tradition's own class
   name (yakṣa, jinn) is a member, not the umbrella.

## 2. The hypernym: *ruho* and *ruhoyen*

**The problem.** *ruho* (Arabic *rūḥ*) carries the soul, spirit beings, the Holy Spirit and
morale/ethos. In a compound such as *yesenruho* the morale sense wins ("a wild temperament").
§11 of conceptual_parity.md already moves the soul senses to *hon*, *paku* and a *seya*
compound; its proposed regloss ("a bodiless being") fails for kami and yōkai, which are often
embodied (kitsune, a mountain as *shintai*, Totoro; even jinn have bodies of smokeless fire).

**How the sources draw the line.** The recurring criterion is *subtle or hidden*, not bodiless:

| Source | Hypernym | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Motoori Norinaga | *kami* (broad sense) | anything extraordinary and awe-inspiring, uncanny and evil things included |
| Orikuchi Shinobu | *tama* 霊 | soul-power that becomes *kami* (higher) or *mono* (lower) |
| Chinese | 鬼神, 神靈 | gods and ghosts together; 靈 = numinous efficacy |
| Korean | 귀신 (< 鬼神) | has drifted to "ghost" (a caution) |
| Malay / Indonesian | *makhluk halus*, *roh halus* | "subtle creatures": jin, hantu, place guardians |
| Javanese | *lelembut* | "the subtle ones" |
| Arabic | *al-ghayb*, *rūḥāniyyāt* | the unseen; esoteric "spiritual entities" |

Anthropological hypernyms: Tylor's "spiritual beings" (too tied to bodilessness); Spiro's
"culturally postulated superhuman beings" (neutral, but "superhuman" misfits small beings);
Boyer's "counterintuitive agents" (most testable); Hallowell's "other-than-human persons"
(one level higher: includes animals).

**The *ruho* family by sense** (from memory; to be checked against native Wiktionaries):

| Etymon | soul | spirit beings | morale / ethos / mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arabic *rūḥ* | core | only the dead (*arwāḥ*), esoteric *rūḥāniyyāt*; jinn are not *rūḥ* | *rūḥ al-qānūn*; morale derived: *rūḥ maʿnawiyya* |
| Persian *ruh* | core | *arvāh* (the dead) | morale derived: *ruhiyye* |
| Turkish *ruh* | core | *ruhlar* (the dead, séances) | *ruh hâli* "mood" |
| Swahili *roho* | core; life, heart | weak (*pepo*, *mizimu* are usual) | disposition (*roho mbaya*) |
| Indonesian *roh* | core | *roh halus*: the general class | weak |
| Russian *дух* | partly | *духи*: the general class (*дух леса*) | *дух закона*, morale |

**Options considered.**

- **A.** *ruho* = spirit being; *ruhoya* = morale/ethos (calque of Persian *ruhiyye*).
- **B.** Keep both in *ruho*, as Russian *дух* does (native precedent, but compounds stay ambiguous).
- **C.** A new hypernym root from 靈 (*líng*, *rei/ryō*, *ryeong*, *linh*); strong Sinosphere
  evidence, but it undoes §11, leaves the Abrahamic sources without a word, and *lin-*
  forms crowd *linea*, *linku*, *linxe*.
- **D (recommended).** *ruho* stays a mass noun (spirit as substance, ethos, morale, inner
  depth; *Santo Ruho* as a name); ***ruhoyen*** is the count noun "a spirit being".
  *-yen* "being (any life form)" follows *bioyen* "life form" and *anjilyen* "angel".

**Corpus evidence.** *ruho* occurs 9 times in Doxo and Xwexi, in only three distinct sentences:

| Text | Use | Change under A | Change under D |
|---|---|---|---|
| UDHR art. 1, *fol ruho fe sodarya* | ethos | → *ruhoya* | none |
| Poe, *amikuya de ruho* | inner depth | none | none |
| Halloween, *folo ruho abil na dolantan Dunya* | spirits of the dead | none | → *folo ruhoyen* |

D leaves the UDHR, the most canonical text, untouched. *ruholi* and *ruhologili literatur*
stay with mass *ruho*. The evidence is three sentences: it leans, it does not decide.

**Risk.** *-yen* also forms practitioners (*jumunyen* "sorcerer", *magikayen*, *ibadayen*),
so *ruhoyen* could be read "spiritist, medium". Mitigation: the *-yen* gloss favours "being",
and the medium gap gets its own form (§13).

**Proposed gloss.** *ruhoyen*: a spirit being; a person, embodied or not, normally beyond
ordinary perception (gods, the dead, nature spirits, jinn, yōkai). A transcendent Creator is
excluded where a tradition sets one apart (Qur'an 17:85 makes the *rūḥ* created; *makhluk
halus* means subtle *creatures*); John 4:24 calls God *pneuma*, which the mass noun *ruho* covers.

## 3. The proposed hierarchy

```
yen (te)                   any person, human or not (Hallowell)
└─ ruhoyen                 spirit being, embodied or not, beyond ordinary perception
   ├─ teo                  worshipped; a full or supreme deity
   ├─ [worshipped beings under a supreme God: yazata, òrìṣà, abosom, saints (dulia)]
   ├─ yesenteo             worshipped outside the official order (淫祀, aramitama, village goddesses)
   ├─ X-prabu              lord of a domain: lokaprabu (owner spirit), drevogeoprabu, hewanprabu
   ├─ X-kef                chief of a kind (animal boss) or office in a divine bureaucracy (城隍)
   ├─ [attendants of a god: shinshi, kenzoku, gandharvas, satyrs; anjilyen]
   ├─ [妖怪 root]          anomalous, unworshipped (analogist cosmologies)
   ├─ the dead             xabahu; hungry ghost; bad-death dead
   └─ named classes        peari > ninfa; yaksa; nāga; jini (jinn); elfu; kentawro; dwarfu …
```

Status can change along the cult axis (Komatsu, Yanagita): **[妖怪 root] → *yesenteo* → *teo***
when a being is taken into worship, and back down when neglected or angered (*tatarigami*).

## 4. The anomalous being: 妖怪

**What it is.** 妖怪 (*yāoguài*, *yōkai*, *yogoe*, *yêu quái*) is the generic word in all four
Sinosphere languages for non-human beings that are neither gods nor the dead. 怪 literally
means "anomaly" (Douglas: category-crossers carry danger and power).

**Polythetic features.** A being is more or less 妖怪-like by how many it shows:

1. non-human (Chinese keeps 鬼 apart; Japanese loosely includes yūrei — follow the stricter line);
2. unworshipped, with a status that can change (Komatsu);
3. born of anomaly: age (tsukumogami), transformation (成精), hybridity, liminal places and times;
4. ambivalent or dangerous;
5. local.

**Comparative precedent.** Inoue Enryō's *yōkaigaku* applied the word to Western phenomena;
Mizuki Shigeru's world yōkai encyclopedias class European and African beings as yōkai; CJK
translations render goblins and trolls with 妖怪/妖精. European folkloristics has the
near-equivalent "lower mythology" (Mannhardt's *niedere Mythologie*, Russian низшая мифология).

**Scope.** Fits the lesser beings of folk belief in societies with a pantheon: Slavic leshy,
rusalka, domovoi; Germanic vættir, nisse; Irish *aos sí*; Malay hantu; Filipino tikbalang;
Mesoamerican chaneque; Indic yakṣa and bhūta. **Excludes:** animist ontologies (nothing is
anomalous where every being is a person), created kinds of revealed theologies (jinn,
angels), the unique monsters of heroic myth (Medusa, Grendel: *kayvutu*), and the dead.

**Register.** Chinese 妖怪 is demonic (subdued by gods, as in *Journey to the West*);
Japanese yōkai is ambivalent and often playful; the pop catalogue (Toriyama Sekien → Mizuki →
*Yo-kai Watch*) flattens it. Anchor the gloss in folk belief, not the catalogue.

**Word form.** A root built on 妖怪 (*yao* alone is taken: "bite"); *yaogway* is only an
illustration. Rejected alternatives:

- ***yesenruho***: reads as "wild temperament" (§2).
- ***ajaburuho*** (*ajabu* "strange"): "weird", not "uncanny".
- ***yesenyen***: *yesen* is also glossed "savage", so "savage person", a colonial slur.
- **精** (*jīng/sei/jeong/tinh*): drifts to "fairy" (妖精 *yōsei*, 요정); 精灵 already glosses *elfu*/*peari*.
- **jinn** as umbrella: Islamocentric; a created race, not transformed beings.
- **yakṣa** as umbrella: a member class (§9), and its living meaning is "ogre".

**Word-list correction.** Remove 妖怪 from the Chinese column of *kayvutu*, which keeps 怪物 "monster".

## 5. *yesenteo*: the unofficially worshipped

*yesenteo* "wild god" fails as the 妖怪 word: Chinese sets 妖 *against* 神, and Chinese pairs
野 with 鬼 (孤魂野鬼), rarely with 神 (to be checked by corpus count); calling jinn or leshy
"gods" breaks parity; and the compound can read "god of the wild" (Pan, Artemis). It fits a
narrower, real concept: deities worshipped outside or against the recognised order — 淫祀
"illicit cults", *aramitama* kami that are placated, South Indian village goddesses before
Sanskritisation.

## 6. Owner spirits: *prabu*, not *suyayen*

**The type.** The most widespread animist category, named in many unrelated families by the
ordinary word for "owner/master":

| Family | Word |
|---|---|
| Turkic | *iye* / *eyesi* (Tatar *su iyäse* "water owner"); Yakut *ičči* |
| Mongolic, Tungusic | *ezen* / *ejen* (*gazryn ezen* "owner of the place") |
| Uralic | Finnish *haltija* |
| Eskimo-Aleut | Inuit *inua* "its person" |
| Slavic | *хозяин леса* (the leshy); the domovoi as house-*khozyain* |
| Japanese | 主 *nushi* (森の主, 沼の主, Kohaku-nushi, Okkoto-nushi) |
| Thai | *chao thi* เจ้าที่ "lord of the place" |
| Amazonia, Mesoamerica | *dono*, *dueño del monte*, *dueño del cerro*; *-jara* |

The relation is mastery with care and exchange (Fausto 2008; Willerslev 2007): hunters
negotiate with the owner of the game. It is not legal property.

**Why not *suyayen*.** *suyayen* (*su* + *-ya* + *-yen*) "owner" is property-holding
(所有者, Esperanto *posedanto*).

**Why *prabu*.** Glossed "lord, lady, master, mistress"; Chinese column 主人, the same character
as *nushi*; sources Hindi प्रभु, Telugu ప్రభువు, Indonesian *prabu*. Its use for God (Doxo:
*Prabu* in the Babel passage and prayers) mirrors the sources: 主 (the Christian Lord and the
master of a swamp), Arabic *Rabb* and *rabb al-bayt*, *prabhu*, German *Herr*/*Hausherr*. The
domain modifier disambiguates: bare *Prabu* = God; X-*prabu* = master of X.

**Forms** (proposals): ***lokaprabu*** "place-lord", the slot name (calque of *chao thi*,
*gazryn ezen*, 土地の主); ***drevogeoprabu*** "forest-lord" (*хозяин леса*, 森の主);
***hewanprabu*** "animal-lord" (master of animals); water word + *prabu* (沼の主, *su iyäse*).
Refer to them with *te*; prefer domains no human holds title to. The word list has no
"landlord" and no *prabu* compound yet.

## 7. *kef*: chief of a kind, or a divine office

X-*kef* compounds name an officeholder over an institution or territory: *xaherkef* "mayor",
*estatokef* "governor", *dawlakef*, *firmakef* "CEO", *navikef* "captain", *eskolkef*; the only
spirit precedent is *anjilkef* "archangel". *drevogeokef* would read "head forester". Two
narrower uses fit exactly:

1. **Chief of its own kind**: the pond's *nushi* as its oldest, biggest fish or serpent;
   Hultkrantz's "animal boss" of a species (fish-*kef*, bear-*kef*).
2. **Divine bureaucracy**: 城隍, the city god as the magistrate's otherworld counterpart (a
   celestial *xaherkef*); 土地公 as the lowest local official (Wolf).

Division: *prabu* = lord of a domain (authority plus care, open to exchange); *kef* = chief of
a kind or officeholder. This matches the ethnographic split between owner of a place and
master of a species.

## 8. Worshipped beings under a supreme God: the yazatas

Avestan *yazata* "worthy of worship" (*yaz-*; cognate Sanskrit *yajata*, *yajña*): created by
and subordinate to Ahura Mazdā, headed by the Amesha Spentas; elements (Ātar, Anāhitā), cosmic
beings (Mithra, Tishtrya), personified abstractions (Rashnu, Sraosha, Vohu Manah); really
worshipped (the Yasna, the Yashts). *teo* contradicts Zoroastrian monotheism; *anjilyen*
(Parsi English renders *yazad* "angel") ignores that yazatas are worshipped.

| Tradition | Supreme being | Worshipped subordinates |
|---|---|---|
| Zoroastrian | Ahura Mazdā | yazatas, Amesha Spentas |
| Yoruba | Olódùmarè | òrìṣà ("diffused monotheism", Idowu 1962) |
| Akan | Onyame | *abosom* |
| Chinese popular religion | the Jade Emperor | the celestial bureaucracy |
| Theistic Hinduism | Brahman / Viṣṇu / Śiva | devas |
| Catholicism | God (*latria*) | saints, angels (*dulia*) |

**Gaps:** this slot itself, and a word for **veneration** (*dulia*); Globasa has *ibada*
"worship" and *santoyen* "saint". Personified abstractions (Fides, Nike, Maat, Vohu Manah) are
a modifier, not a slot.

**Cautions.** Avestan *daēva* "demon" vs Sanskrit *deva* "god", still alive in Persian/
Turkish/Urdu *dev*/*div* (ogre) vs Hindi *dev* (god): no loan from this family is possible.
*peari*'s etymon drifted (Avestan *pairikā*, a malign being → Persian *parī*, benevolent); the
modern sense governs, but the etymology note should say so.

**Soul set for conceptual_parity §§7–12:** *urvan* (judged at the Činwad bridge → the *seya*
compound), *fravaši* (pre-existent guardian soul, venerated at Farvardigān), *daēnā*
(conscience, met as a maiden at the bridge), *ahu* (life → *jiwa*).

## 9. Named classes

**Nymphs and *peari*.** Nymphs are owners of particular places (naiad, dryad, oread; the
hamadryad dies with her tree) with real local cults (Larson 2001). Female, young and
marriageable is a feature bundle (*fem-*), not a slot. Cross-cultural cousins: apsarases
(Urvaśī), *tennyo* (*Hagoromo*), 織女, *vila*, Mami Wata, *pari/peri*; the swan-maiden tale
links many. Proposal: ***peari*** = a usually female nature or celestial spirit, often
marriageable to mortals; ***ninfa*** = the Greek class under it; mark "fairy" (the small winged
Victorian fairy) as drift.

**仙.** 仙女 in the Chinese column means a female 仙, a Daoist transcendent (a human who reached
immortality): a separate concept (*seon*, *sennin*, *tiên*) and another gap.

**Yakṣa.** Wider reach than 妖怪 (Sanskrit *yakṣa*, Pali *yakkha*, Hindi, Bengali, Sinhala
*yakā*; Thai *yak*, Khmer *yeak*, Javanese *yaksa*; 夜叉 *yèchā*, *yasha*, *yacha*, *dạ xoa*),
but it names one class beside rākṣasa, bhūta, preta, nāga, gandharva, and its living meaning
is "ogre" (Thai, Khmer, Javanese giants; Sinhala demon; 母夜叉). Proposal: its own entry
***yaksa*** (free: neighbours *yaku* "yak", *yaxu* "beast"), the dated example of the
worshipped–feared continuum (Coomaraswamy).

**The Buddhist eight classes** 天龍八部 (deva, nāga, yakṣa, gandharva, asura, garuḍa, kinnara,
mahoraga), hypernym *amanuṣya* 非人: a native pan-Asian source of class loans. **Do not calque
"non-human"**: Japanese 非人 *hinin* was an Edo outcast status and is a slur.

**Other lower beings by slot:** household spirits (domovoi, brownie, *nisse*,
*zashiki-warashi*; *lares* toward *yesenteo*) → house-*prabu*; attendants (satyrs and maenads,
gandharvas, kinnaras, Inari's foxes 神使/眷属; angels) → attendant slot; hybrids outside a
retinue (centaurs, woodwose, 野人) → 妖怪 slot + classes (*kentawro*); water beings split by
origin (rusalka = bad-death dead; kappa = 妖怪; nāga = class; Mami Wata = worshipped;
nixie/*nøkk* = water-*prabu*); elves, *aos sí* = classes on the fallen-gods pattern.

**Parity finding.** The word list has elf, dwarf, troll, centaur, unicorn and nymph but not
nāga, apsaras, garuḍa, tengu or jinn.

## 10. Animist ontologies

Descola's four ontologies (animism, totemism, analogism, naturalism) scope the vocabulary: 妖怪
belongs to analogism; animism needs words for persons and relations.

1. **Other-than-human persons** (Hallowell): Globasa marks animacy in grammar (*te*/*to*,
   *ete*/*oto*, *-pul*), and *yen* covers a "personified object". Animist texts keep *te*
   for a mountain or a stone; the te/to stage in the pipeline must not "correct" it.
2. **Owner spirits**: §6.
3. **Ancestral beings** (Dreaming; Descola's totemism): creators still present in the land;
   neither *lefeatre* "ancestor" nor gods. A gloss rather than a coinage, for now.
4. **Transmissible power**: *mana*, 靈, *baraka*, *manidoo*, *wakȟáŋ*, *orenda*. Globasa
   *baraka* is glossed only "blessing"; Arabic, Swahili *baraka*, Turkish *bereket*, Hausa
   *albarka*, Malay *berkat* also mean a holy power passed between people, places, objects:
   restore that sense.

## 11. Indigenous North American beings: Coyote and the windigo

1. **Trickster** (Radin 1956; Beidelman's critique of overextension): Coyote, Raven, Iktomi,
   Wisakedjak, Anansi, Èṣù, Hare, Kancil, Māui, Loki, Reynard, Sun Wukong, kitsune/tanuki.
   *ilusi* "deceive" gives only the deceiver; a coinage or class anchor is needed.
2. **Culture hero / transformer** (Boas): Nanabozho, Glooscap (not a trickster), Xa:ls,
   Māui, Prometheus, Fuxi/Shennong. Forms with *hero* or *aloforma* + *-yen* (proposals).
3. **Myth-time people** ("when animals were people"; Eliade's *illud tempus*): capitalised
   ***Koyote*** with *te* for the myth person, *koyote* for the animal, as Doxo does with *Prabu*.
4. **Personal guardian / dream visitor**: Ojibwe *bawaagan*, vision-quest helper, *genius*,
   *daimōn*, Siberian helping spirits, guardian angel. A gap.
5. **Cannibal being**: *wiindigoo*/*wìhtikow*, Stone Giants, Arabic *ghūl*, rākṣasa,
   *yamauba*, Baba Yaga, Grendel. Globasa has *tamlam* "greed", *yamkalya* "hunger",
   *yamfilya* "gluttony" but no being; a *ghūl* loan would cover the Arabic–Persian–Turkish–Urdu
   world, with the windigo as a class under it.
6. **Human-into-monster transformation**: windigo, *oni*/*kijo* (Hannya), werewolf, 成魔, the
   bad-death dead; the reverse of 成精. *aloformacu* describes it; the scheme marks direction.
7. **Personified condition**: the windigo as winter starvation; Famine, Plague.
8. **Emic/etic**: "windigo psychosis" (Marano 1982; Brightman 1988): gloss the being in the
   community's terms, never by the etic label.

**Ethics.** Many nations restrict telling (Coyote and windigo stories in winter only); the
Diné treat the skinwalker as not to be discussed: keep it out of examples. Use published,
community-sanctioned sources and community spellings (*wiindigoo*, *wìhtikow*, *Wenabozho*).

## 12. Test cases

**Miyazaki** (native Japanese categories on screen): Totoro 森の主 and Okkoto-nushi/Kohaku-nushi
→ *prabu*; Nago's *tatarigami* → a kami degrading toward the 妖怪 slot; the stink spirit →
pollution and purification (*kegare*); Shishigami → master of animals near *yesenteo*; kodama,
susuwatari (精霊 *seirei*) → *yen* in the animist sense; No-Face → 妖怪 slot; the bathhouse's
八百万の神 → *teo*. 神隠し *kamikakushi* is a gap: abduction by spirits (changelings,
*bergtagning*, *engkanto*). The title もののけ shows drift: classically a possessing spirit
(Lady Rokujō's *ikiryō*), repurposed by Miyazaki for wild animal gods; gloss the classical sense.
The English reception ("gentle Ghibli spirits") is not a category.

**Avatar: The Last Airbender** (an American synthesis written in English): a disambiguation test
for eng→glb, since every "spirit" must resolve into *teo*, X-*prabu*, the 妖怪 root, *ruhoyen*
or *hon*. Hei Bai → wrathful *prabu*; Koh → 妖怪 slot; Wan Shi Tong → owner of the library; Tui
and La → *teo*; Yue → apotheosis; Raava/Vaatu → *teo* or principles; dark spirits →
*tatarigami* pattern; Aang's spirit journeys → *hon* (Eliade's soul flight); the solstice →
liminal time. Gaps: the **otherworld** (Irish Otherworld, 常世), the **recognised reincarnate**
(*tulku*, *khubilgan*; not the Sanskrit *avatāra*, a god's descent), and **apotheosis**
(城隍 as dead officials, Guan Yu, *goryō* such as Sugawara no Michizane → Tenjin).

Both film corpora are under copyright: short quoted lines for analysis only, nothing committed.

## 14. Tooling: the Word Selector field sweep

Built in this session to collect such vocabulary without an English pivot (uncommitted):

- **`word_selection/field.py`** finds headwords whose *own-language* glosses in the native
  Wiktionary editions (ja, zh, de, fr; es, ru, ko once built) name a term: ja:妖怪 finds かっぱ
  ("日本の妖怪の一つ"), さとり, やまびこ, あやかし, 天狗; de:Geist finds Banshee, Katzengeist, Lemur,
  and compound heads (*Naturgeist*, *Waldgeist*) bring Alb, Elb, Faun, Erdmännchen.
  English Wiktionary is never searched. **G** marks hits whose gloss is defined by the term
  (genus); the typed terms' own entries are listed as tickable rows.
- **Spreading:** a term also searches other editions through the seed's own translation table,
  only via a sense another typed term confirms (or the only sense), and through Han spelling variants.
- **Speed:** a gloss cache per edition in `word_selection_cache/` (31 s once for four
  editions); a sweep takes 0.5–1.6 s (mmap `find` with byte boundary checks; a lookbehind regex
  took 9 s).
- **Page:** "From a semantic field" panel (sweep, genus filter, + sweep per hit, Untick all,
  Copy list TSV); "Untick all / Tick all" above the Sources table.
- **Fixes:** English *yokai* now comes in through "cognates found by etymology", which follows
  borrowings *from* a gathered word (ja 妖怪 → en yokai); zh.wiktionary tables' mixed-script
  romanizations ("ようかい, yōkai") are reduced to Latin, with a labelled fallback to the entry's
  own romanization.
- **Limit:** no Arabic, Persian, Turkish or Hindi editions are built, so jinn, *pari* and
  yakṣa vocabulary only arrives through ja/zh/de/fr glosses; building ru (*дух*) matters most next.
