Victorian–Globasa Dictionary

Victorian English words and senses that trip a modern reader, each with its Globasa rendering. Seeded from H. G. Wells, The Time Machine (Heinemann, 1895). Every sense is attested in the text and checked against two period translations and three period dictionaries.

Layers. Period trap: a modern reader misreads or no longer knows the sense (dated sense, or Webster 1913 marks the word archaic or obsolete). Rare word: still current but unfamiliar. Technical: a scientific term.

Evidence. Amos = E. W. Amos's Esperanto translation (about 1938); d'Albroi = J. d'Albroi's Spanish translation (1925/26); a ✓ means the translator's word matches the gloss of the Globasa rendering, and a dotted word is an unconfirmed alignment. Holt = the US first edition (1895). Rhodes (1908), O'Connor & Hayes (1907) and Velázquez (1910) are period bilingual dictionaries; Webster = Webster's Revised Unabridged (1913).

Apertium. A sense marked preference is used by the English→Globasa pair when AP_SETVAR=victorian is set; the modern translation stays the default otherwise. A sense marked eng→glb entry is always available.
