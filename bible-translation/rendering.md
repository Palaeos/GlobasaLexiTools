# Globasa renderings

How Hebrew constructions are rendered in the Globasa draft. The Hebrew-side
analysis these rely on, with sources and page references, is in the
Hebrew/Greek project's `docs/translation-criteria.md`.

**Aims**

- **Readable and direct:** a translation made with modest effort, in the
  spirit of Zamenhof's Esperanto Bible.
- **A base for a scholar to edit:** every machine choice keeps its basis
  note and its alternatives.

## Clause linkage (Isaksson 2024)

| Hebrew | Globasa |
|---|---|
| wayyiqtol chain | `ji (S) V` (bare verb; see Tense in narration) |
| wayyiqtol → wa-X-qatal (background or contrast) | `ji`; `mas` for an explicit contrast; background state bare, earlier event `le` (see Tense in narration) |
| several X-qatal in a row (simultaneous) | no "then" |
| Ø-participle after wayyiqtol | bare finite verb (the dictionary form also covers the progressive), `nun` for explicit progressive, or `dur na V`; not `-ne` with an object |
| וְהִנֵּה + participle / verbless clause | `ki …` after the perception verb |
| verbless clause after wayyiqtol (background) | its own sentence |
| וַיְהִי + time clause, then wayyiqtol | `Denwatu hu S V, …` bare; for "after" `Xaki S V, …` |
| protasis + weqatal | `Eger …, (ji) xa …` |
| weqatal chain in instructions | `(musi/am) …, ji …` |
| weqatal + wa-lō-yiqtol (result) | `ji fe folo no …` (result); `celki … no …` (purpose) |
| new paragraph (discontinuity clause, then wayyiqtol) | paragraph break |

| לְמִינוֹ "according to its kind" | 1:11–25 (10×) | `fol` | extension: לפי, על פי |
| קָרָא לְ "call X Y" (naming) | 1:5, 1:8, 1:10 | Ø: `namegi X Y` | Doxo "namegi mi day Rita" |
| לֵאמֹר "saying" (quote) | 1:22 | Ø: comma and quotation | Doxo dialogue |

- **The machine lexicon gets these wrong.** SDBH lacks entries for על,
  אל and the inseparable prepositions, so `Gen_1_glb.tsv` falls back to
  Modern Hebrew homographs (על → `yugo` "yoke"). Prepositions are chosen by
  these rules, not by the lexicon.
- **Extension gaps:** `fe`'s Hebrew column (אֵצֶל) is wrong for time; `tas`,
  `intre`, `kom` have no Hebrew. The Globasa word list's English and
  Esperanto columns decide there.
- **Role vs comparison:** "as X" in the sense of a role or function is
  `fe rol fe X` (word list: "(acting) as, in the capacity of"); `kom` only
  compares ("like X").

## Construct chains: `de` or `fe`

Hebrew's construct state does not distinguish possession from
description; Globasa does. `de` = "of, belonging to" (possession); `fe` =
"of (relating to)", "as an equivalent of `-li`", preferred for complex
descriptive phrases (grammar: *Function Words* § Fe; *Word Formation*,
Descriptive Noun Compounds: "hadya fe xencudin"). The choice is
interpretive and recorded per phrase.

## Tense in narration

- **Narration has no past tense.** Globasa tells a story with the bare
  dictionary form, "as if the scene of a film were being described, in the
  present tense" (Xwexi, *Verb Forms*, Omission of Verb Particles). So
  wayyiqtol and narrative qatal get no `le`. Doxo's folk tales narrate this
  way (Goldilocks: "Denwatu hu te fini, Dahabutofa hisicu yampul").
- **Aspect is marked relative to the narrated now.** The compound tenses
  combine a vantage particle with an aspect particle: `nun` "progressive
  (active)", `le` "perfective (completed)", `xa` "prospective"; `ja-` adds
  immediacy (*Verb Forms*, Compound Tenses). In narration the vantage is the
  narrated now and stays unmarked, so the aspect particle stands alone. The
  full compounds (`nun le`, `le le`, `nun nun` …) do not occur in Doxo
  (0 in ~48,000 words); `jale` and `jaxa` do ("Dahabutofa jale xorsomno
  denwatu hu baru ruata"; *Tell-Tale Heart* "mi le jaxa buka lenterna").

| Hebrew | Aspect | Globasa in narration |
|---|---|---|
| wayyiqtol storyline | completed, in sequence | bare |
| stative (1:2 הָיְתָה) | state | bare |
| participle, ongoing (1:2 מְרַחֶפֶת) | progressive | bare (the dictionary form covers it); `nun` only to make it explicit |
| wa-S-qatal background, earlier event, and the wayyiqtols continuing it | completed before the now | `le`; `jale` for "had just" |
| הִנֵּה + participle of imminence | prospective | `jaxa`; `xa` |
| iterative yiqtol (Gen 2:6) | habitual | `du-` |
| time clause, simultaneous or sequential | — | bare: `Denwatu hu S V, …` |
| time clause, earlier event ("after he had …") | completed before | `Xaki S V, …` (the conjunction carries the anteriority); `Denwatu hu S le V` only for a plain "when" with an earlier event |

- **Subordinate clauses stay bare in narration.** The grammar makes tense
  markers obligatory in `eger`, `denwatu hu`, `durki`, `koski` clauses, but
  in narration the narrated now counts as the established tense: all 24
  `denwatu hu` clauses in Doxo's folk tales are bare. `le` there would mark
  anteriority, so it is used only where the Hebrew means that. Not `u`: it
  marks the bare predicate in dictionary-style citation, not narrative
  (project owner).
- **Anteriority needs a Hebrew marker.** Use `le`/`jale` only for a
  wa-S-qatal background clause and the wayyiqtols that continue it (Isaksson,
  *The Verb in Classical Hebrew*, pp. 218–220: Gen 31:34 "Rachel *had
  taken* the idols and put them … and sat on them").
- **No harmonising pluperfect.** A wayyiqtol without such a marker is
  sequential: Gen 2:2 `fini` (on the seventh day God finished), Gen 2:8,
  2:19 bare. The pluperfects of NIV and others ("by the seventh day … had
  finished"; 2:19 "had formed") harmonise: 2:2 with God resting on the same
  day (already felt by the LXX, Samaritan and Peshitta "sixth"), 2:8 and
  2:19 with the order of Gen 1 (the P/J doublet). They are recorded as
  alternatives (`le fini`, `le …`), labelled as harmonisations.
- **Other forms:**
  - attributive participle with an object (1:11–12 מַזְרִיעַ זֶרַע, עֹשֶׂה
    פְּרִי) → relative clause `hu da V …` (relative clauses need no marker).
    `-ne` adjectives "cannot function as verbs" and take no direct object.
  - passive participle (בָּרוּךְ) → `-do` adjective.
  - jussive → `am` (3rd person: "should/must"); cohortative → `Imi am …`.
- **Direct speech keeps its own tenses.** Inside a quotation, markers
  follow the speaker's time (`le`, `xa`, `jale` …), as in Doxo's dialogue
  ("Bante le yam bankwanti to of misu yamxey!").

## The conjunction *wa*

*Wa* is a natural-language connective whose reading is "ambiguous and
pragmatically determined" (Isaksson, *The Verb in Classical Hebrew*, pp.
79–80): "then", "so", "at the same time", "so that", "if … then". It is not
translated as a fixed `ji`.

| *Wa* link (Isaksson) | Gen 1 | Globasa |
|---|---|---|
| verse-initial, new storyline step | most verses | new sentence, no connective (Doxo: sentence-initial *Ji* in 84 of 2,337 sentences); the pipeline drops it (`prep_stream.py`) |
| background clauses (discontinuity) | 1:2 (p. 143 n. 48) | no connective before the first ("Now …"); `ji` between them |
| storyline resumed after background | 1:3 ("Then God said", p. 125) | `Jaxali,` (Doxo 54×), if 1:1 is read as a main clause |
| fulfilment of a command | 1:7 ("So God made", p. 37 ex. 19) | `Kos to,` (Doxo 36×) |
| result of a command | 1:3 "and there was light" | `ji` |
| *wa* + jussive continuing a command | 1:6 ("and let it separate") | `ji … am` |
| *wa*-yiqtol after a jussive: purpose | 1:9 ("so that the dry land appears", p. 236 ex. 117) | `celki` + noun + infinitive |
| *wa*-qaṭal in a modal series: intention | 1:14 (p. 466 n. 17) | `ji … am` |
| same-event addition with fronted object | 1:5 ("and the darkness he called Night", p. 377 ex. 92) | `ji` + Fronting rule |
| elaboration without *wa* | 1:27 (p. 494 ex. 9) | semicolons, no `ji` |
| between nouns | "the heavens and the earth" | `ji` |

## Speech, possessives, set phrases

- **Speech:** `loga: “…”` (Doxo: `loga:` 78, `loga,` 57). לֵאמֹר "saying" →
  `, loga:`.
- **Possessives:** `sesu` only refers back to the clause's subject; otherwise
  `tesu`/`etesu` (animate), `tosu`/`otosu` (inanimate); in relative clauses
  `dasu` (grammar). "God set the light … according to *their* kind" →
  `etesu`; the tree "according to *its* kind" in its own relative clause →
  `sesu`.
- **וַיְהִי־כֵן** "and it was so" → `Ji to okur denmaner` (`denmaner` adv "like
  that"; not `denpul`, adj "that kind of").
- **הִנֵּה** "behold" → `Am oko,` (Doxo).
- **"all that"** → `moyto hu … da` (Doxo).

## Fronting and focus

| Hebrew | Default | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| fronted object, contrastive or unclear | `S V he X` (plain SVO, `he` on the object) | `be-` passive; `el` O-S-V |
| fronted object, given referent, no contrast cue | `be-` passive `X beV S` (agent as bare object) | `S V he X`; `el` |
| casus pendens (topic shift) | `tem X, … te/to …` | mirrored `X, … te/to …` |
| time or place frame | fronted with a comma (`Fe xoru, …`; `Fe perya de sui, …`) | — |
| subject-first / thetic | plain SVO | — |
| unnecessary subject pronoun | `he` + pronoun | — |
| רַק / אַךְ + fronted element | `sol` | — |
| indefinite fronted before לֹא / אֵין | `nil` + noun | — |
| contrastive focus / English *it*-cleft | `he` + word | — |
| verb-final in prose, S-O-V | `S V he O` | — |
| verb-final in prose, S-PP-V | `S, PP, V` | — |
| verb-final in the poetic books | `S el O V` (keeps chiasmus) | prose rule |

Usage notes:

- **"Given"** is computed within the selected source strand (P, J, …).
- **Corpus check** (Globasa doxo texts, 16k words): `el` appears 2×, both
  in one poem; `be-` with an overt agent appears 2 of 70 times.

## Relative clauses, parenthesis

- **Relative clauses:** `hu … da`, including bare relatives
  (`<@FS-N←>` / `<@FS-NP>` in the source stream). `da` takes the place of
  the Hebrew resumptive pronoun.
- **Parenthesis** (Holmstedt 2020; only *inside* a host clause):
  - short insertions: `( … )`;
  - mid-clause insertions such as נְאֻם יְהוָה: dashes.
  - Asides between complete sentences are background, not parenthesis.

## Voice

Niphal, pual and hophal are not mapped mechanically to `be-`:

| Rendering | Use |
|---|---|
| `be-` | passive |
| `-cu` | "becomes / gets" |
| `-gi` | causative |
| plain verb | middle |

The choice is made in lexical selection, from context. Every `be-` records
whether it comes from fronting (active Hebrew verb) or from a passive-type
stem.

## Lexicon

Generated by `build_glb_candidates.py` from the source side's lemma table.

- **Sense:** SDBH's sense is chosen by passage, using SDBH's references.
- **Candidates:** SDBH's English, Spanish, French and Chinese glosses matched against
  the word list and the Wiktionary extension; the extension's (Modern) Hebrew
  column matched against the lemma corroborates. Notation and ranking: README.
- **Cross-checks:** BDB and Klein.

Flags:

- **"several SDBH senses in this passage":** e.g. Gen 1:2 רוּחַ "wind" /
  "Spirit", אֱלֹהִים "God" / "mighty". These become reading alternatives.
- **Capitalised divine glosses:** never the default (parsimony). They are
  offered as alternatives. This concerns the choice of *sense* (e.g. רוּחַ
  "Spirit" vs "wind"), not the spelling of God's name.
- **The divine name is `Yahwe`** (project owner): יְהוָה is rendered as a
  name, not as a title ("LORD", Kyrios, Adonai). יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים (Gen 2–3)
  → `Teo Yahwe`. This keeps the name/title difference the source-critical
  reading of Gen 1 (אֱלֹהִים) and Gen 2–3 (יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים) depends on.
- **God is `Teo`.** אֱלֹהִים referring to the God of Israel is written
  `Teo` (project owner; Doxo: "O Teo!", "fe Teo"). Other gods, and אֱלֹהִים
  in a generic sense, are `teo`. The lexicon prints the lowercase word-list
  form.

**Curated senses** come from the source side's `data/lexical_readings.tsv`
and are mapped to Globasa in the `glb_alternatives` column. A curated sense with
status `preferred` becomes the default, and the SDBH sense is kept as the
alternative. To switch back, set the line's status to `alternative`.

### צֶלֶם and דְּמוּת "image" and "likeness"

| Hebrew | Globasa | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| צֶלֶם | `imaje` "image, picture" (Epo *bildo, imago*) | A concrete, relational noun: a made image *of* someone. Cult images (Num 33:52; 2 Kgs 11:18), figures (1 Sam 6:5, 11; Ezek 23:14); metaphorically a phantom or dream-image that still keeps the idea of shape (Ps 39:7; 73:20; Clines, *TynBul* 19 [1968] 74–75). All 17 uses are "image of" someone (van Wolde 2026, p. 44). Akkadian *ṣalmu* "statue, image", an image that makes the god present (van Wolde p. 45, citing Berlejung and Bahrani). |
| דְּמוּת | `simya` "similarity" | Resemblance, appearance (van Wolde p. 44 n. 99). Kept distinct from צֶלֶם: the 9th-century Tell Fekheriye statue calls itself both *ṣlm* and *dmwt*; Garr (*IEJ* 50 [2000]) reads *dmwt* as the king's relation to his god, *ṣlm* as his presence before his subjects. |
| בְּצֶלֶם | `in` | As ambiguous as the Hebrew. |
| כִּדְמוּת | `kom` | Comparison. |

- **Why `imaje`:** it is the general concrete word, like English *image*,
  Latin *imago*, Greek *eikōn*: it covers statue, picture and phantom and
  leaves open what the image consists of. Readings of that are
  interpretations and stay alternatives:
  - `fe rol fe` / `wakilyen` "representative": functional or royal reading;
    Clines' beth essentiae "as our image".
  - `figura` "shape, figure": bodily-form reading (Skinner).
  - van Wolde's metonymic presence needs no change of word.
  - `soxo` "statue": the background of *ṣalmu*, too narrow for Gen 1; notes
    only.
- **Not** `simya` for צֶלֶם (that is דְּמוּת's word), **not** `wakil`
  "representation" (builds in the functional reading).
- **Keep the words and prepositions fixed** across Gen 1:26–27, 5:1–3 and
  9:6, so that Gen 5:3 stays visible: there the prepositions swap,
  בִּדְמוּתוֹ כְּצַלְמוֹ "in his likeness, as his image" → `in sesu simya, kom
  sesu imaje`. Gen 1:27a: `… in sesu imaje, in imaje de Teo …`.

### בָּרָא in Gen 1:1–2:4a (van Wolde 2026)

The project owner prefers van Wolde's reading. Its latest form
(*JHS* 26 [2026] 1–55, doi:10.5508/jhs29692) makes the sense depend on the
construction (§4.4, pp. 34–36), so the rendering is chosen per occurrence:

| Verse | Construction (van Wolde) | Her sense | Globasa |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | Type 1, plural object (heavens, earth) | separate a whole into spatially disjoint parts (p. 14) | `julagi` |
| 1:21 | Type 1, three groups of animals | separate, each to its own life sphere (pp. 15–16) | `julagi` |
| 1:27ab | Type 2, singular object + בְּ | set the human being apart (from the other living beings) in his image (p. 51) | `julagi` |
| 1:27c | Resultative, plural | "he separated them into male and female" (p. 33) | `julagi … cel` |
| 2:3 | Type 2, singular | the rest set apart from all making work (p. 23) | `julagi` |
| 2:4a | Niphal infinitive, plural | "when they separated" (middle, resultative; p. 38) | `julacu` |

- **One sense-based rendering.** van Wolde's reading is a single sense of
  separation with both "separate" and "set apart" (project owner), so ברא is
  `julagi` in every verse; `vibaja` "divide" is not used for 1:27c.
- **Why `julagi` covers both types:** its Esperanto gloss *apartigi* means
  "make apart", which is both "separate" and "set apart" (van Wolde's types 1
  and 2). The English column says only "separate", so the candidate script
  cannot find it from "set apart". The choice rests on the Epo column.
- **Not בדל's glosses:** van Wolde contrasts ברא with בדל, the separating
  done by the heavenly bodies (p. 14); בדל keeps its own entry (`julagi` too, but from SDBH).
- **Alternatives:**
  - `krea` "create": SDBH, BDB, Klein; the traditional reading.
  - `kostrui` "construct": Becking & Korpel, *JHS* 10 (2010), art. 3. They
    read ברא as a late, deliberately non-anthropomorphic verb for building,
    chosen over בנה, and rule out "separate" (no preposition; no verse
    requires it). Reply: van Wolde & Rezetko, *JHS* 11 (2011), art. 9;
    van Wolde 2026 n. 22 (against Wardlaw, *VT* 64 [2014] 502–13).
